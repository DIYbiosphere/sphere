One motivation for establishing DIYbio.org in advance of widespread activity was to help create frameworks for best practices worldwide. Amateur and hobbyist communities formed around ham radio, astronomy, chemistry, meteorology, rocketry, and personal computing all developed ethical codes, norms of practice, and shared resources that helped their communities succeed. DIY biology has followed the same path — see the [Code of Ethics] for the movement's own norms.

From 2013–2018, DIYbio.org ran "Ask a Biosafety Expert," a volunteer program connecting the community with a biosafety advisory board, in partnership with the Woodrow Wilson Center's Synthetic Biology Project (funded by the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation). That program has since closed, but the resource list it curated remains useful for anyone doing biology outside a traditional lab setting.

## General references

- **[American Biological Safety Association](https://www.absa.org/)** — its Biosafety Links page is a good starting point for further reading.
- **CDC's [Biosafety in Microbiological and Biomedical Laboratories](https://www.cdc.gov/labs/bmbl/index.html)** (6th Edition) — the field's standard reference, technical but thorough.
- **[NIH Office of Science Policy — Biosafety and Biosecurity Policy](https://osp.od.nih.gov/policies/biosafety-and-biosecurity-policy/)** — publishes the NIH Guidelines for Research Involving Recombinant or Synthetic Nucleic Acid Molecules, a highly technical regulatory document, but its FAQ and educational materials are more approachable.
- **[Health Canada Pathogen Safety Data Sheets](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/laboratory-biosafety-biosecurity/pathogen-safety-data-sheets-risk-assessment.html)** — safety data sheets for a wide range of infectious agents, both common and exotic.

Some of these links have moved or been reorganized since they were first collected — if you find a dead link or know of a resource worth adding, [open an issue][new issue].
